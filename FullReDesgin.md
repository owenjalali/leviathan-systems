# Leviathan Systems — Website Redesign Doctrine
## Stripe / Linear / Webflow Standard

---

## 0. The Root Truth (Non-Negotiable)

Great websites do not persuade.
They **signal**.

Persuasion happens *after* trust is silently established.

Stripe, Linear, and Webflow do not:
- convince
- explain
- pressure
- dramatize

They position themselves as **inevitable infrastructure**.

If a website sounds like it wants the user,
it has already lost status.

Leviathan Systems must feel like:
> This already exists. Decide if you’re ready.

---

## 1. The Core Problem With the Current Leviathan Site

The current site is:
- copy-led instead of perception-led
- accusatory before authoritative
- text-heavy with weak visual proof
- trying to win an argument instead of asserting existence

This creates the impression of:
> A smart agency trying to persuade me

Instead of:
> A system already used by serious companies

This is not a tooling problem.
This is not a Claude problem.
This is a **taste + hierarchy problem**.

---

## 2. What Elite SaaS Sites Have (Psychological Mechanics)

Before a user reads a single word, their brain asks:

1. Is this real?
2. Is this built by people smarter than me?
3. Is this for companies like mine?

Stripe, Linear, and Webflow answer all three **visually**, not verbally.

They rely on:
- restraint
- spacing
- calm typography
- density control
- silence

Authority is visual first.
Words come second.

---

## 3. Why Starting With Pain Fails

Opening with:
- “You’re losing leads…”
- “Your business is broken…”
- “You’re doing this wrong…”

fails because it **accuses before earning authority**.

High-status companies never open with diagnosis.

They open with **identity**.

Pain comes **after** authority is established.

---

## 4. The Correct Mental Model for Leviathan

Leviathan Systems is **infrastructure**, not a pitch.

Infrastructure:
- does not shout
- does not explain itself immediately
- does not persuade emotionally
- does not try to convert everyone

Infrastructure simply exists.

The website’s job is not to maximize conversion.
Its job is to **filter for seriousness**.

If small or unserious businesses bounce:
good.

---

## 5. Homepage Structure (Correct Order)

### Section 1 — Hero (Status Filter)

Purpose:
- Establish authority
- Signal inevitability
- Filter the audience

Rules:
- 70% visual
- 30% text
- Minimal language
- Declarative tone

Hero copy principles:
- No “We help…”
- No “AI-powered…”
- No “You’re losing…”
- No urgency
- No hype

Hero text should state:
- what this is
- why it exists
- without blame or drama

The hero visual is **non-negotiable**.

It must show one of:
- a real system diagram
- a live-looking dashboard
- a flow: lead → response → booked → revenue

If the visual is weak, the copy will never carry authority.

---

### Section 2 — System Overview (Silent Proof)

Purpose:
- Show Leviathan is a system, not a service

Format:
- One clean system diagram
- Minimal labels
- No paragraphs
- No feature lists

Let the system speak for itself.

Observation > explanation.

---

### Section 3 — Loss Calculator (Pain, Now Earned)

Purpose:
- Introduce pain only after authority exists

Reframe:
- Quantification, not fear
- Math, not persuasion
- Diagnosis, not blame

The calculator should feel like:
> Measuring latency

Not:
> Scaring the user

---

### Section 4 — Why This Exists (Belief Layer)

Purpose:
- Explain the philosophy briefly

Constraints:
- 1–2 short statements maximum
- No storytelling
- No dramatization

Core ideas only:
- Speed compounds
- Humans introduce latency
- Latency kills inbound economics

---

### Section 5 — Outcomes (Abstracted Implementation)

Purpose:
- Show results without revealing mechanics

Do NOT list:
- tools
- APIs
- integrations
- tech stacks

Show:
- response time decreases
- booking rate increases
- lead decay eliminated

Outcome > feature.

---

### Section 6 — CTA (Confident, Soft)

Purpose:
- Invite exploration, not force action

No:
- “Book now”
- “Get started”
- urgency language

Better:
- “Run the system check”
- “View response architecture”
- “See how it works”

Confidence replaces urgency.

---

## 6. Visual Language Rules (Taste Specification)

### Typography
- Neutral, modern sans-serif
- No playful fonts
- Large headings
- Tight body copy

### Color
- Restrained palette
- One accent color maximum
- No loud gradients
- Subtle contrast only

### Spacing
- Aggressive whitespace
- Clear section separation
- Nothing crowded

Whitespace signals confidence.

---

### Motion
- Slow
- Intentional
- Minimal

Motion should feel like:
> A system initializing

No gimmicks.

---

Claude is good at:
- text
- logic
- structure

Claude is bad at:
- taste
- restraint
- hierarchy
- knowing what not to say

Claude defaults to:
- marketing language

Elite SaaS defaults to:
- understatement
- implication
- confidence through silence

Claude must be **constrained**, not asked politely.

## 8. Claude Prompt That Forces Taste

Use this exactly:

You are designing a homepage for a high-status infrastructure company.

Rules:
- Do NOT use marketing language.
- Do NOT explain benefits explicitly.
- Do NOT use fear, urgency, or pain in the hero.
- Reduce total copy by at least 50%.
- The site must feel inevitable, expensive, and boring in a good way.
- Assume the reader is intelligent and skeptical.

Visual priority:
- The hero must be 70% visual, 30% text.
- The visual must show a real system, not abstract shapes.
- Copy must be declarative, not persuasive.

Tone:
- Calm
- Confident
- Minimal
- Precise

Goal:
- Filter for serious buyers.
- Signal infrastructure, not services.
- Establish authority before introducing pain.

If something feels like marketing, remove it.
