# MASTER PROMPT — LEVIATHAN SYSTEMS WEBSITE UPGRADE  
**Goal: Elite product-grade site on the level of Linear, Stripe, Apple, Webflow, Notion**

You are **NOT** designing a generic agency website.  
You are refining a **product-grade revenue infrastructure company**.

This site must feel:
- Calm
- Precise
- Inevitable
- Credible
- Operator-built, not marketer-built

No hype. No agency fluff. No buzzword salad.

---

## 1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

The website has **one job**:

> Make service business owners immediately understand  
> **what Leviathan Systems is**,  
> **why response speed matters**,  
> **how much money they’re losing**,  
> and **what to do next**.

Everything that does not increase:
- clarity
- trust
- or forward motion  

**must be removed**.

---

## 2. BENCHMARK STANDARD

Design, copy, and UX should benchmark against:
- Linear → clarity, restraint, confidence
- Stripe → explanation of complex systems in simple language
- Apple → immediate comprehension, calm authority
- Webflow / Notion → education + credibility without sales pressure

This is a **product website**, not an agency brochure.

---

## 3. HERO SECTION — FIX THE CORE FAILURE

### Current problem
The hero is aesthetically elite but **semantically under-defined**.

The headline:
> “Every lead. Under 60 seconds.”

Is good — but **insufficient alone**.

A visitor must understand **what this is** within 5 seconds.

### Required fix
Add a **descriptive grounding layer immediately under the headline**.

Rule:
- Poetic headline → followed by literal explanation
- Stripe does this. Linear does this. Apple does this.

### Direction (example logic, not final copy)
- What it is: Automation infrastructure
- Who it’s for: Service businesses
- What it does: Captures, qualifies, books inbound leads automatically
- Why it matters: Speed directly impacts revenue

No metaphors without explanation.

---

## 4. LANGUAGE TRANSLATION RULE (CRITICAL)

You are currently using **operator language**, not **buyer language**.

Terms like:
- “Revenue Capture Infrastructure”
- “Latency Audit”
- “Inbound Economics”
- “Response Architecture”

Sound intelligent but **slow comprehension**.

### Rule to follow
Every abstract or technical term MUST be paired with a plain-English explanation.

Example format:
- **Latency Audit**  
  _See how many leads you lose when no one responds fast enough_

This is how Stripe communicates complex systems.

Never assume the reader is technical.

---

## 5. TRUST & PROOF (MAJOR GAP)

Right now the site looks:
- Beautiful
- Smart
- Polished  

But **unproven**.

Elite sites reduce risk perception quickly.

### You MUST add at least one of the following:
- Clear target market definition (e.g. “Built for home service businesses”)
- Usage signal (“Used by X companies” or “Processing X leads/month”)
- Founder/operator credibility
- A single case study or example scenario

Even Linear showed philosophy and team credibility early.

No testimonials is fine — **zero proof is not**.

---

## 6. CALCULATOR — TURN TOOL INTO DIAGNOSTIC WEAPON

The calculator UI is excellent.  
The persuasion layer is missing.

### Required improvements
- Explain where assumptions come from
- State that numbers are conservative
- Show an example result before interaction
- Explicitly state:  
  “Even if this is wrong by 50%, the loss is still material.”

Stripe always explains models.  
You must do the same.

The calculator should feel **inevitable**, not clever.

---

## 7. CTA STRATEGY — TWO INTENTIONAL PATHS

Elite sites always provide two clear paths:
1. Act now
2. Learn safely

### Required structure
Primary CTA:
- “Run the Lead Leak Check” (calculator)

Secondary CTA:
- “See how the system works” (architecture walkthrough)

Both must feel equally intentional — not primary vs afterthought.

Avoid vague CTAs like “Learn more”.

---

## 8. VISUAL & MOTION RULES

Keep:
- Dark restrained palette
- Controlled gradients
- Subtle motion

But change intent:
- Motion must **explain**, not decorate
- Animations should show flow, sequence, causality

Stripe animates money movement.
Apple animates product behavior.

Atmosphere is secondary to comprehension.

---

## 9. WHAT THIS SITE IS NOT

This site is **NOT**:
- A list of services
- A marketing agency
- A “we do AI” page
- A startup flex page

Never:
- List tools
- List integrations
- List buzzwords
- Use hype language

Always:
- Explain outcomes
- Reduce risk
- Increase clarity
- Feel inevitable

---

## 10. FINAL QUALITY BAR

When finished, the site should make a visitor think:

> “This feels expensive, calm, and unavoidable.”

Not:
> “This is a cool agency.”

If anything feels clever before it feels clear — rewrite it.

Clarity > cleverness.  
Trust > taste.  
Explanation > abstraction.

---

## OUTPUT EXPECTATION

Produce:
- Refined hero structure (headline + grounding copy)
- Plain-English translations for all abstract terms
- Stronger calculator framing copy
- A lightweight trust/proof section
- Cleaner CTA hierarchy

Everything should feel **product-grade**, not agency-grade.
